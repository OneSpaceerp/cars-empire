8.6 Advertising UX
The sponsored banner must feel native to the Cars Empire visual language but remain clearly distinguishable as a sponsored placement.
On mobile, use a wide card/banner with rounded corners, strong offer value, merchant identity and a single primary CTA. Avoid tiny text that cannot be read at normal viewing distance.
On web, support responsive widths and retina/high-density assets without changing the slot contract.
When no advertisement is available, remove the component entirely; no blank card, placeholder, reserved margin or broken image may remain.
If an ad is loading, use a lightweight non-layout-shifting placeholder only when necessary; after NO_AD the placeholder must collapse completely.
The ad destination must open in the same app/web context whenever possible and preserve campaign attribution through the subsequent offer/order flow.
Add an optional "Sponsored" label and merchant logo. Never label an organic offer as sponsored.
Respect dark mode, RTL Arabic layouts, safe-area insets and accessibility contrast.
Advertising-specific non-functional requirements:
Ad decision API p95 target: <= 150 ms from cache for standard traffic.
No ad response may block primary page rendering; fetch ads asynchronously when technically appropriate.
CDN cache creatives aggressively while keeping campaign eligibility server-controlled.
Ad delivery must remain available even if analytics collection is temporarily degraded; tracking must fail open for page rendering.
Campaign budget and billing mutations must be strongly transactional and idempotent.

10. Security, Privacy & Fraud Controls
Encrypt all traffic with HTTPS/TLS.
Hash passwords with Argon2id; never log passwords or OTPs.
Tokenize payment instruments through PSPs; do not store sensitive card data.
Use signed webhooks and idempotency keys for every payment event.
Rate-limit OTP, login, voucher validation and redemption endpoints.
Use short-lived access tokens and rotating refresh tokens.
Add merchant-device binding or MFA for high-risk actions.
Enforce branch scope and staff scope server-side.
Protect against double redemption through row-level locking/transactional constraints.
Use fraud rules for repeated redemption, abnormal refunds, device/account clusters and suspicious merchant behavior.
Log all manual state overrides with actor, reason, before/after values and timestamp.
Provide privacy controls for location, marketing and service history data.
10.1 Advertising Security & Fraud Controls
Sign or server-validate billable impression/click events; never trust the browser/mobile client to set chargeable amounts.
Use bot/device heuristics, abnormal click-rate detection, repeated-click throttling and campaign-level fraud review.
Merchant cannot modify campaign billing events or reported spend.
Admin overrides to campaign status, budget or billing require an audit reason.

12. Acceptance Criteria
1. Consumer can register via OTP and create a vehicle in < 2 minutes.
2. Consumer cannot purchase an offer that is incompatible with selected vehicle rules.
3. A booking slot cannot be double-booked when capacity is exhausted.
4. A successful captured payment creates exactly one order and exactly the intended number of vouchers, even if client retries.
5. A voucher cannot be redeemed twice concurrently.
6. A merchant staff member can redeem a voucher in <= 3 screens after login.
7. A redeemed service creates or links to exactly one work order for the primary service entitlement.
8. Additional work requires explicit customer approval and records actor, timestamp and amount.
9. Refunds move money state and entitlement state consistently and are fully auditable.
10. Merchant settlement totals reconcile against captured transactions, refunds and commission.
11. Admin overrides require permissions and reason codes.
12. Every critical financial or entitlement mutation has an idempotency strategy.
13. Customer sees transparent final price before payment.
14. Customer can access purchased voucher offline after successful payment.
15. Completed work produces a persistent vehicle service record visible to the customer.
12.1 Advertising Acceptance Criteria
16. GLOBAL_TOP_BANNER is available as a configured consumer app/web slot on all standard consumer-facing pages.
17. When no campaign is eligible, the ad component is completely absent and consumes zero layout height.
18. When a campaign is eligible, only an approved, scheduled, funded and targeting-matched creative can be served.
19. A merchant can create, pay for, submit and monitor an advertising campaign without Admin database intervention.
20. Admin can approve/reject/pause campaigns and audit every manual action.
21. A campaign supports city/category/vehicle/audience/time targeting and frequency caps.
22. Impressions and clicks are idempotent and billable only from server-authoritative events.
23. Merchant performance includes impressions, clicks, CTR, spend, remaining budget and attributed conversions where enabled.
24. A paid campaign cannot serve after end_at, budget exhaustion, policy suspension or failed payment.
25. Campaign creative and destination are validated before approval.
26. Ad rendering never blocks checkout, voucher viewing or critical consumer actions.
27. Ads are clearly identified as sponsored content when required by policy.

14. Recommended Engineering Decisions
Use PostgreSQL as system-of-record for transactional data.
Use Redis for cache, short booking holds and rate limits; never as the authoritative store for money or voucher state.
Use an event bus/queue for notifications, analytics, settlement jobs and non-critical asynchronous workflows.
Use object storage for vehicle/work-order photos and KYC documents.
Use OpenSearch/Elasticsearch for bilingual automotive search.
Use a payment abstraction layer so PSPs can be replaced or expanded without rewriting checkout.
Use a modular monolith for MVP if the team is small; split services only when scale or ownership justifies it.
Keep money, entitlement and redemption logic in strongly transactional boundaries.
Use Arabic/English from the first schema and API version: localized fields, search normalization and RTL-ready UI.

Final architectural principle: Cars Empire should own the customer relationship, transaction state, entitlement/voucher state and service history while allowing merchants to own the operational execution of the service.
Appendix A — Cars Empire Advertising Product Definition
Commercial principle: advertising is a first-class revenue stream. Cars Empire sells scarce, measurable placement inventory to automotive merchants while preserving a clean organic marketplace. The global slot is always structurally available but visually absent when there is no eligible paid campaign.
Recommended initial product name in the merchant portal: "Sponsored Banner Campaign". Internal slot key: GLOBAL_TOP_BANNER. Future slots may include CATEGORY_BANNER, SEARCH_SPONSORED_CARD, MERCHANT_SPOTLIGHT and CHECKOUT_CROSS_SELL, but those should be introduced only after the first slot is proven.
Reference image requirement: the supplied screenshot demonstrates the intended interaction pattern: a prominent promotional visual with merchant branding and a strong offer, embedded naturally into the consumer home experience. Cars Empire should use automotive imagery, service-focused value propositions and its own brand system rather than copying the reference artwork.
Appendix B — Product Benchmark Note
This specification intentionally adopts selected marketplace mechanics observed in the Waffarha benchmark — offers, merchant onboarding, location discovery, payments, vouchers, merchant redemption, personalization, wallet/loyalty and B2B2C distribution — but reinterprets them for automotive services. Cars Empire adds vehicle garage, vehicle eligibility, scheduling, work orders, technician workflows, inspection/add-on approval and service history as first-class domain objects.
